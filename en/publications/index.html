Paper Highlights

Cost-effective scheduling of a hydrogen-based steel plant · Applied Energy, 2025

The technical feasibility of the hydrogen-based direct reduction — electric arc furnace (H2-DRI-EAF) route has been demonstrated by pioneering projects, yet the electricity cost of water electrolysis remains the dominant component of operating cost. Conventional process simulation tools are well suited to parameter optimisation but cannot readily represent task dependencies among the four tightly coupled subprocesses: water electrolysis, hydrogen storage, shaft-furnace direct reduction and EAF steelmaking. Economic assessments also tend to omit short-term operational constraints, so the resulting schedules may not be feasible in production.

This study builds a separate resource–task network (RTN) model for each of the four subprocesses and integrates them through boundary dependency constraints, while explicitly modelling hydrogen, direct reduced iron and electricity balances, task start-up and duration, and material transfer times. With the plant supplied jointly by local wind power and the grid, the model co-optimises task scheduling, power levels and grid purchases, turning hydrogen production from a fixed load into a flexible load that follows wind availability and time-of-use prices; the hydrogen storage decouples production from consumption over time. Case studies show that total operating cost falls substantially across production scales without additional equipment investment, and the integrated RTN formulation offers a transferable modelling paradigm for deeply coupled industrial processes.

Multi-objective steelmaking scheduling with storage · Journal of Cleaner Production, 2023

EAF steelmaking is electricity intensive, so both electricity cost and indirect grid emissions constrain operation. Existing RTN scheduling models usually represent electricity consumption only and omit indirect emissions from the optimisation objectives; the effect of order processing time requirements (PTR) on make-span is also commonly overlooked. The mismatch between batch production and the time resolution of electricity prices and renewable output further limits the granularity of demand response.

This study develops an extended RTN multi-objective mixed-integer linear programming model that embeds both electricity consumption and indirect carbon emissions, together with key process constraints such as heat precedence and continuous casting. A what-if analysis defines representative make-span scenarios, and the normal boundary intersection (NBI) method generates evenly distributed Pareto-optimal solutions for each scenario, allowing the plant to select a cost–emissions balance consistent with the actual PTR. A battery energy storage system compensates for the coarse time granularity of batch production.

Case studies based on actual UK electricity prices and renewable output data compare the most relaxed and the most restrictive processing time requirements: combined electricity and emission costs fall by up to 68.5%, indirect carbon emissions by 83.5%, and renewable self-consumption rises by 12.1%. The results indicate that offering a choice of Pareto solutions under varying make-span requirements, and using storage to refine time granularity, are central to deeper participation of the steel industry in demand response. Further work may address robust scheduling under equipment failures and order changes, participation in ancillary service markets, and coordinated scheduling across multiple plants.
